# Idea: redactionprint

A **redactionprint** is the impression a poem leaves when its words are held back.

Under the current masking rule, every word becomes the same glyph. Punctuation, spaces, line breaks, stanza breaks and the public title remain. The result carries a visible pressure-pattern: how many verbal units gather in a line, where the breath catches, where a mark interrupts, where a stanza releases, where a shape returns.

Something like the poem's fingerprint.

The compound earns both halves:

- **redaction**: the lexis is withheld under custody;
- **print**: a residual impression is made public. A print as output, trace, pressure-transfer, and perhaps fingerprint.

It also names more than punctuation. *Punctuation score* makes the marks sound like the whole object and carries a performance instruction the artefact may not intend. *Redactionprint* leaves the spacing, lineation, recurrence and block-shape inside the name's field.

## Two kinds of fingerprint

The redactionprint is a **human-readable fingerprint**. It lets a reader see family resemblance, structural difference and some revision marks without receiving the words.

The SHA-256 beside it is a **cryptographic fingerprint** of the canonical private text. It identifies one exact byte sequence; deterministic regeneration and byte comparison tie that sequence to the public redactionprint.

They work together and do different jobs. A redactionprint may collide with another poem's shape; it does not prove identity. A matching hash verifies byte identity with practical certainty and carries almost no appreciable sense of the poem. One can be looked at. The other can be checked.

## What travels

- token count and distribution, without token length under the one-glyph-per-word rule;
- punctuation and its positions;
- line, stanza and whitespace topology;
- any deliberately retained typographic markers;
- the possibility of comparing punctuation- or structure-level revisions;
- a public trace to which a custody record and private-vault pointer can attach.

The words do not travel through this face. Information still does.

## Possible forms

- **redactionprint** — the resulting artefact or medium;
- **redactionprinting** — the masking and impression-making operation;
- **`redactionPrint()`** — the executable generator when the code-facing register matters.

## Working glosses

The operator's own explanations on the day of the coinage:

> "a bit like a fingerprint, but I suppose less human, and a bit like a hash, but less, uhm forensic. It is supposed to retain the identity and convey something of its structure, rhythm and 'pressure', without disclosing any of the rest of its content."

> "it kinda allows you to show and talk about a poem without completely giving up first publication rights, and it is a bit like looking at a poem through a foggy bathroom window."

These are working descriptions, not claims of legal certainty or unique identification. The first holds redactionprint between bodily identity and cryptographic verification. The second names both its practical custody aim and the partial, obscured encounter it offers.

## Into painting

Later the same day, the operator stated the next practical direction:

> "I intend to use some redactionprints in paintings B-)"

That closes only the question of whether the form may enter the painting practice. Which poems, and by what material operation, scale or degree of legibility, remain open.

## And-Yet

A fingerprint can be mistaken for an essence. The poem exceeds this trace: voice, sound, semantic pressure and lived address remain elsewhere. The redactionprint is one face under a custody decision, something like a fingerprint, with *something like* kept load-bearing.

Redaction also carries censorship, state secrecy and institutional power. Here it may protect first-publication possibility and the operator's right to withhold. The older charge does not disappear merely because the hand doing the redacting is the author's own.

## Not yet

- Whether different masking rules produce different redactionprint species: one glyph per word, length-preserving bars, syllabic marks, typographic blocks, or something else.
- How redactionprints enter paintings: support, transfer, masked layer, compositional measure, or something not yet named.
- Whether **redactionprinting** and **`redactionPrint()`** become working terms or remain possibilities attached to the noun.
