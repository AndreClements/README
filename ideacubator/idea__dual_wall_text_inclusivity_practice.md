# Idea: Dual Wall Text as Inclusivity Practice in the Arts

**Status:** seed
**Origin:** Solo exhibition, March 2025; formalised here February 2026
**Related:** `projects/theses/parametric_authorship.md` (multi-register strategy, §6 Pedagogy)

---

## The Practice

Present **two equally-weighted wall texts** for every exhibited work:

1. **Full-density text** — gallery-speak, theoretical vocabulary, unlimited conceptual compression. For the reader who wants the complete signal.
2. **Translated text** — the same underlying meaning, filtered through a "Fanagalo translation" into beginner-to-intermediate English. Not a summary or dumbing-down, but a genuine second rendering: plain vocabulary, shorter sentences, concrete metaphors. Equally weighted in display (same size, same prominence, same wall).

The name "Fanagalo translation" references the South African pidgin — a lingua franca built for cross-boundary communication under constraint. The method borrows the impulse (bridge across registers) without borrowing the specific language.

## Why It Matters

- **Inclusivity without condescension.** The second text is not a "simple version." It's an alternative vehicle for the same invariant. Both are first-class.
- **Against institutionalism.** Gallery-speak can function as gatekeeping. A dual text says: "the ideas are for everyone; the vocabulary is a design choice, not a prerequisite."
- **A new avenue for artistic expression.** The act of translating between registers is itself creative work — it surfaces what the ideas actually *are* when stripped of disciplinary jargon. Sometimes the translated version is better.
- **Parametric authorship in practice.** The dual text is a concrete instance of the thesis's multi-register strategy: same rule manifold, two generated vehicles. The invariant is the concept; the transition is the register.

## Potential Paper

This practice could be formalised and promoted as an **inclusivity methodology for exhibition and publication**. Possible structure:

- Document the March 2025 exhibition as case study (visitor responses, engagement patterns)
- Formalise the "Fanagalo translation" method as a repeatable protocol
- Position it against existing museum/gallery accessibility practices (audio guides, "easy read" labels)
- Argue that dual-register presentation is not accessibility-as-afterthought but a **primary authorial act**
- Connect to parametric authorship: the constraint (write in two registers) produces richer meaning than either register alone
