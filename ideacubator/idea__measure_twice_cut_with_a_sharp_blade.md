> **Measure twice, cut with a sharp blade. How many cuts? Depends.**

## Against the proverb

The carpenter's version is *measure twice, cut once*. Both are about caution. The original's *cut once* claims a certainty it can't have, as if measuring twice guaranteed the cut. The axiom keeps the caution and takes it further and deeper: into the readiness of the tool and the hand, the one part that can actually be prepared. Its third sentence asks the count and gives it back to the situation: the material, the work, the moment.

(Gloss from the operator's correction, 2026-10-10; the machine's first reading had it as a move from counting to decisiveness, and was wrong.)
