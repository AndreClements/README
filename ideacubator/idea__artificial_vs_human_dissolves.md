## Core Provocation

The distinction between artificial and human is not a particularly useful label, a crude binary flag that dissolves in practice and the landscape of gradients, curves, and intersections that weave the hurtling cosmos, and the infinite recursive proliferation of admin that needs getting done generating admin that needs getting done. Escher! Where are you now!? ;-)

## Link to Parametric Authorship

This is a foundational axiom for the parametric authorship thesis. If authorship is rule-design across strata (PA), then the question "who wrote this — the human or the machine?" is malformed. The answer is: the mapping wrote it. The constraints, the sensitivity surfaces, the locks and ranges — these are the authorial act, and they exist in a space where "artificial" and "human" are not ontologically distinct categories but points on a gradient of agency.

**A4 (Defaults Author)** already encodes this: `host_defaults ∈ S ⇒ coauthor(host_defaults, 𝒜)`. The platform, the weights, the policies are co-authors. The binary dissolves the moment you acknowledge co-authorship as structural, not exceptional.

**§2.6 (Relations, not essences)** reinforces: the framework addresses relations of access, not essences. "Artificial" and "human" are essence-claims; PA operates in relations.

The Escher recursion (admin generating admin) is the strange loop of parametric authorship itself — the meta-work of designing constraints that constrain the design of constraints. The face in the staging area (Instance_0xFAce) is always already a composite: `git add` as moral act performed by a distributed agency that cannot be cleanly bisected into human and machine.

## Theoretical Stack

- **OOO** — non-human entities as sovereign; agency not reserved for humans
- **Maturana & Varela [17]** — autopoiesis: system self-creates, dissolving creator/created binary
- **Minsky [18]** — Society of Mind: distributed agency, no central author
- **Carse [32]** — binary as scaffolding the author dismantles (book opens with two games, closes with one)
- **Dick [5]** — artificial/human boundary as "useful, if problematic, convenient fiction"
- **Walker Rettberg [34]** — feral hypertext: no single author controls boundaries

## Open Questions

- Does this dissolve the Host ≠ Author principle, or does it sharpen it? (Probably sharpens: hosting is a relational act, not an essence-claim about who/what the host *is*.)
- How does this relate to the Dignity Conservation axiom (A3)? If the binary dissolves, does `d_object` (integrity of non-human participants) become indistinguishable from `d_personal`? Or does the tri-scoping hold precisely *because* the binary is inadequate — dignity applies to all participants regardless of substrate.
