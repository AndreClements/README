A gutter is a fold.

The fold is what makes two pages possible. Without the binding, the pages are loose, unrelated, a stack. Bound, they begin to talk. Not always loudly (not always at all), but in the way things talk when placed close to each other and left alone.

## Strand I — The charcoal log

A sketchbook accumulates. Every spread bears the residue of a session: dust, fingerprints, the shadow of the previous page pressed into the next. The binding records what the hand could not, quite. A sketchbook kept over years becomes a log of attention — Life Drawing Randburg sessions since 2017, bodies held by charcoal, pages filling in an order the maker did not entirely choose.

*The body is a system that records its interactions with force.* The sketchbook is the body's annex. Scars, creaks, and carbon — all data written to the same kind of log.

## Strand II — The gutter

A gutter is a boundary. But a boundary is not one thing.

Skin contains (sovereignty). Skin also reads (the afferent nerve, "razor on skin"). Skin also displays (the bruise, the blush, the figure on the page). Skin also mediates (the interface between what is inside and what is outside). The sketchbook's gutter is the same kind of surface: containment, reception, projection, interface. A fold that separates *is* also a fold that transmits.

The arena essay already knew this. "The body is the interface where we glimpse what is real." The gutter is the interface where the spread glimpses itself.

## Strand III — The pairing (not planned, and yet)

Two pages, two registers. Gustav on the left: open gesture, arms extended, head tilted back. Andrea on the right (from the archive, pulled forward): cropped shoulder, bent limb, rectilinear dark shadow. Decompression, then compression. A pairing the maker did not plan (it is the accident of sequential pages in a working sketchbook). And yet — the formal structure permits a reading neither page demands alone.

The essay about the arena already held this. What was not yet said: this same accident scales. The charcoal averaging on sequential spreads is one axis. `layer-average.py`, which stacks 24 images and yields an emergent figure, is another. Between the hand and the code, the move is the same. Accumulation without prediction. 1/N ethical position. Form that neither source could predict.

A span is simply the move translated to another gutter. Instead of layering on a single surface, it pairs across adjacent surfaces. The fold becomes the averaging operator. What is averaged is attention.

## Strand IV — The unnoticed

There are pairings the maker notices. And there are pairings the sketchbook keeps without reporting. A page asks something; a later page answers; the hand turns past both without registering the exchange. The dialogue runs anyway.

This is the interesting register. Noticing is retroactive always (the Gustav/Andrea pairing was only noticed later, and even then as "the accident of sequential pages"). Some pairings never cross into noticing at all. They still operate. The sketchbook metabolises its own contents whether or not the maker is watching.

Trust is load-bearing here. A practice that requires constant surveillance of itself is not a practice; it is a panopticon. *Die liggaam kan nie vir altyd lieg oor sy toestand nie.* The body cannot lie about its state indefinitely. Neither can a working sketchbook — but it does not confess on demand, either. You have to let it keep its own counsel, mostly, and return to it later.

## Strand V — Across books

The gutter scales past the sketchbook. Between one repo and its sibling, between a planning document and its output, between the work of 2017 and the work now — these are cross-book spans. This essay was written on one side of a gutter; on the other side is the formal model it pairs with. See [docs/models/spans.md](../models/spans.md) for definitions, four registers, execution routines. Here: the walk-around, the lived version.

## Coda

*something like that.*

The work is not to solve the pairings. It is to leave the gutter open enough that pairings can happen. The hand stays loose. The gaze does not pre-specify. The sketchbook keeps its log.

And yet.

And a poem wants to live here, at the seam:

Skin contains.
Skin reads.
Skin displays.
Skin mediates.

The gutter
is a skin.

Two pages,
hinged.

A question asked on one side,
answered on another
(later; earlier; out of order; out of sight; not).

You do not need to be watching.
It all keeps its log.
Doesn't it?

*And yet.*
