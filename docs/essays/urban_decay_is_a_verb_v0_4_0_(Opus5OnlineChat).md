# Urban Decay is a Verb

*v0.4.0 · a photo-essay · thirteen plates · Johannesburg, and elsewhere, 2012–2026*

_The pictures are not illustrations. Several of them are the argument, and the text is what I owe them._

---

The garbage trucks stopped coming, and when they came back the whistles were beautiful. Nothing about the trucks had changed.

That is the first trap in the word decay. It pretends the thing itself has failed.

> **Plate 1 · `IMG_1278_urban_decay_is_a_verb`**
> *Urban Decay is a Verb.* Joe Slovo Drive, Johannesburg. A Sunday morning, 2023. PowerShot G1X MkII, through the vehicle window.

A pedestrian staircase smouldering by itself on a Sunday morning, shot through a car window. The window stays in the picture. We will come back to the window.

Not the staircase burning. Cable burning under it: insulation cooked off for the copper. The smell is what tells you, and only if you have lived somewhere it was ordinary.

The photograph knows less than the body did. It gives concrete, bright noon, smoke, the stair as scarred geometry, green pushing down over the top edge, some small red thing in the dust that insists on being seen without agreeing to mean anything. It does not give the smell. It does not give the few hundred rand, or the hands, or why the hands needed to.

So the text has to carry what the frame cannot. That is the whole job.

---

## Banked

A city's mass is banked relation. Grids, girders, reticulation, 4am shifts poured as concrete and left to set. Somebody surveyed that corner. Somebody costed that span. Somebody carried a theodolite up a hill in 1936, and somebody has been paying for it ever since, in instalments nobody itemises.

That is what is standing there. Not stone. Held decisions.

> **Plate 2 · `Life(ASClem_2015_07_29_1293)`**
> *Life.* Johannesburg, 29 July 2015, 21:33.

Decay is not the mass leaving. It is the field withdrawing (capital, concern, the weighing) while the structure stands like a score nobody sings. Some of the windows still light. The lease is still on somebody's books at a number that stopped being true around 2009.

> **Plate 3 · `04_crop_colour_3200`**
> Johannesburg CBD, 19 March 2017, 02:22.

And the city is not empty, which the empty pictures forget to mention. Two people on a corner under a pink sign at twenty past two in the morning. A tower down the street with most of its floors lit. Somebody is selling foam offcuts. This is a working town at an hour when the pictures usually claim it is a carcass.

## The whistle

Weight arrives. That is the part nobody warns you about.

The trucks came back, and the whistle went through the yard, and it was the best sound of that year. Record the same man whistling at the same bin in a good month and you would have heard nothing at all. The sound had not improved. We had.

Nothing was added to the truck. What came back was the weighing.

> **Plate 4 · `_MG_0800`**
> Burnt flatbed, highway outside the city, 22 June 2016, 01:45.

It runs the other way in the same season. A flatbed burnt to its frame at twenty to two in the morning, its load cooked across two lanes in wrapped packets, the pylons still walking over the hill behind it, indifferent and fully operational. Somebody withdrew a service. Somebody burned a truck. Somebody's grievance was real, and the vehicle it landed on was a working flatbed with a number plate.

The truck is the same object in both sentences.

## Harvest

Decay is not a noun, and the verb has a subject.

Under the staircase, the cable. Insulation cooked off in a shallow gully, copper lifted out, copper sold. That is not weather. That is somebody's Sunday spent crouching in smoke for a few hundred rand and a burn on the forearm.

Call it what it is: the configuration dismantled to liberate the substance. Making, run backwards, at a far worse rate. Everything that went into laying that cable (the trench, the survey, the shift, the sign-off, the whole banked argument) cashed out at scrap by a person who never saw a cent of the original.

*Verval.*

Noun and verb in the same six letters, and Afrikaans declines to tell you which one you are getting.

Some subjects are easy to name because they arrive wearing municipal logos, development plans, quarterly reports. Some are harder because they arrive as need. The person burning cable is not the field that made copper worth burning and a city worth leaving. To collapse them is dishonest. To pretend they do not touch is also dishonest.

> **Plate 5 · `MagNieGeboggelWordNie(StandertonElektries_2016)`**
> Standerton, 5 June 2016, 00:19.

A carriage on a dead siding, stripped to its ribs, roof flaking under electric yellow. Along the flank the stencil is still perfectly legible: an instruction, in Afrikaans, about how this vehicle may not be buffered or loose-shunted. The rule outlived the institution that meant it. Nobody obeys it. Nobody has bothered to deface it either. It goes on being a rule, on the side of a thing, in the dark.

> **Plate 6 · `DiepslootRefuseFire(ASClem_2015_10_08_1344_take2)`**
> Diepsloot, 8 October 2015, 22:50.

And the fire, always the fire. Refuse burning in a donga at ten to eleven at night, with the streetlight above it working perfectly.

That last fact is the one to keep. Nothing in the frame permits the clean story. The light works. The fire burns. A field can stay bright in one place while withdrawing one metre away, and the city is good at this. So are we.

## The window

Now the window.

It is in the frame at the top and again at the bottom, and it is slightly greasy. Four seconds would have cropped it out.

It is there because some months earlier I had gone in on foot and been beaten for the camera: Hillbrow, the early hours of the second of January. The camera went. One tripod leg came home. I posted the shiners.

One night is not a life. The sentence stops there.

What I had gone out to photograph that night was darkness. Specifically: the parts of the city blacked out by load-shedding, the grid withdrawing on a schedule published in advance, a field switched off by timetable. I got good material on the outskirts, and then the inner city did what the inner city does to me, and I went in. Early mornings are in some ways the safest hours to work in these spaces, provided you hold situational awareness, visibility, distance. I let all three go for a composition.

> **Plate 7 · `school_hillbrow`**
> Johannesburg, undated. A camera on its tripod on the pavement, screen lit. A school's board overhead, GDE registration and two numbers; a phone number for an abortion pasted to the pole below.

That is the last frame of that night, and it is a phone snapshot of the camera that was about to be taken. The screen is blazing. Both hands were busy. The evidence of the trade is in the picture, which I did not compose and have never improved on. The street had already written the caption.

So: to photograph a ruin is to weigh it in a field the people inside it cannot afford. Say so, or the picture lies. The window is me saying so. It is not a defence. It is a declaration of position, and it costs the frame something, which is the point.

## The boys

> **Plate 8 · `Zandspruit_Ballgame_2012_DSC6875`**
> Zandspruit, 13 June 2012, 15:26.

A road closed by a barricade, and because it is closed it is a pitch. Two boys and a ball on the best open tar in the district that afternoon. Fire low across the lane behind them, smoke into the trees, a crowd along the fence going about the business of being a crowd. The ball is mid-roll. The younger boy is following it the way you follow a thing you are about to be responsible for. The older one has turned, and is looking straight down the lens.

He is identifiable. I will not pretend that is a small thing.

I have written elsewhere that I do not photograph the people of these spaces, and I have written it more absolutely than I hold it. The narrower and truer version: I decline the profit. I decline the frame that converts somebody's worst week into my good year. A child cannot audit that bargain, and the picture will outlive the afternoon by decades, and neither objection goes away.

*En tog.* Avoidance is also an omission. Keep the child out of the argument and you keep childhood out of the city the argument claims to read: the clean unpeopled stair, the empty ruin, a Johannesburg in which nobody plays. That is a quieter picture, and quieter is not the same as kinder.

Context matters, and these boys are not a misfortune I am harvesting. The barricade is behind them and it is not on them. The road did not become a pitch despite the trouble; it became a pitch because of it, and the taking of it is theirs.

And context is the first thing a photograph sheds. Captioned, he is a boy playing ball on a closed road. Uncaptioned, in a room I will never enter, he is evidence of whatever that room already wanted to see. I cannot follow the picture. I can only load it before it goes.

Dignity, then, rather than absence. What does the photograph ask of the person inside it, what does it give back, and would he recognise himself in the sentence I have just written: a young man now, somewhere, who once owned a road for an afternoon. I am leaving that open on purpose, in print, where it can be checked.

## The green

> **Plate 9 · `Durban Deep_2021(FNMBJ_Jul21_MG_0887)`**
> Durban Deep, West Rand, 17 July 2021, 16:37.

Then the green comes in over the top of the frame without permission.

Grass standing in the doorway, a tree through the roof sheet, the plan of the building still perfectly readable underneath, because a wall goes on being a wall long after it stops being a room. Something else has started weighing this, on a scale the original owner never held and could not have read. The termites are enthusiastic. The grass has views.

The green is allowed its beauty. It is not allowed to solve the picture. It might be hope. It might be neglect with chlorophyll.

> **Plate 10 · `FacesinBordeaux_2019_MG_6016e`**
> Bordeaux, France, 26 April 2019, 22:30.

And you start seeing faces in the render. Everybody does. There is nobody in that wall (the wall is old and damp and hung with netting) and yet a face arrives, because a face is what I am equipped to find. What a thing is made of is one question. What is looking is another, and it is the one that keeps moving.

## The gold

> **Plate 11 · `OWN-tower(2014_07_30_1459_tmp)`**
> Johannesburg, 30 July 2014, 22:54.

The gold left long ago and the streets still bend around where it was. Main Reef Road runs where the reef ran. The strung-out shape of this whole place is an ore body nobody has touched in a lifetime, and everything afterwards travels the bent line and calls the bending *the way to town*.

The small ones go the same way. A tower with VIDEO TOWN bolted to the top of it, a rabbit in a top hat, 5 DVD GAMES. Not one part of that has stopped existing. Every letter is present and the paint is honestly not bad. What went is the entire world in which a person drove somewhere on a Friday to rent a film and back on Sunday to return it.

The sign is in excellent condition. Nobody can read it.

## The wall

There is no plate for this one, which is itself the finding.

I grew up in the suburbs, and what I did there was walk. Pick a direction and go: over the wall between one property and the next, along the top of it, down into somebody's yard and out the other side. That was ordinary. A child doing it now would meet barbed wire, an electric strand, a response vehicle, possibly a firearm.

Nobody calls that decay. The houses are maintained. The gardens are watered. Property values have held.

But it is the same event, run the other way. Down there the field withdrew and left the structure standing; up here the field walled up and left the structure standing. Both are relation converted into something else: in one direction abandonment, in the other fortification, and in both a street that used to be crossable is not. The suburb decayed into security, which photographs as nothing at all, which is why I have no plate for it and why the pictures always seem to be about somewhere else.

They are not about somewhere else. I go down there partly because I cannot get out up here.

## Elsewhere

> **Plate 12 · `Midnight(Paris 2019)`**
> *Midnight.* Paris, 4 May 2019, 00:00:41.

A bed on the pavement, made up, pillow squared, clothes folded, occupant absent, bins standing guard. Forty-one seconds past midnight. The camera is precise about this, and the title turned out to be true to the minute.

It is in here because this is not a local speciality. Withdrawal is a thing fields do everywhere, and the wealthy ones do it more quietly, under better lighting, one doorway at a time. Bordeaux, eight days earlier, was a wall coming apart under netting, and nobody calls that a decaying city. It decays at the ordinary rate. It is simply weighed by people who can still afford the weighing.

Point this camera only south and the pictures begin arguing something I do not believe.

## The last frame

> **Plate 13 · `EventHorison(aPinhole-Poem)[pinhole_on_digital-doubleExposure-2017-2026]`**
> *Event Horison (a pinhole poem).* Pinhole on digital, double exposure, 2017–2026.

A pinhole laid over a digital frame, nine years in a single sheet, and it will not resolve. I have stopped wanting it to.

Mass that persists can always be re-weighed, and never only on the original owner's scale. The building does not know it has been abandoned. The street does not know the gold is gone. The copper is worth exactly what the copper is worth.

A ruin can be a wound, a shelter, a resource, a danger, a playground, a studio, a market, an archive, a warning, a home. The same structure, weighed in rooms that will never agree. Concern behaves like weather when you are caught inside it, but it is not weather: it has budgets, names, hands. A field withdrawn by us can be returned by us, and fought over by us, and the fight is the city.

The garbage trucks stopped coming, and when they came back the whistles were beautiful.

Nothing about the trucks had changed.

Everything about the field had.

*Verval.*
