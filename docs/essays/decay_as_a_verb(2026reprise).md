# Seed — Urban Decay is a Verb
**Working masthead, operator's own (2023). v0.0.3 — 2026-07-26. A seed, kept unfurnished. It unfurls as it does.**

The garbage trucks stopped coming, and when they came back the whistles were beautiful. Nothing about the trucks had changed.

A pedestrian staircase smoulders by itself on a Sunday morning, shot through a car window. The window stays in the picture. (The window was there because three months earlier the photographer went in on foot and was beaten for the camera. One night is not a life; the sentence stops there.)

Not the staircase burning. Cable burning under it: insulation cooked off for the copper. The smell is what tells you, and only if you have lived there.

A city's mass is banked relation: grids, girders, 4am shifts poured as concrete. Decay is not the mass leaving. It is the field withdrawing — capital, concern, the weighing — while the structure stands like a score nobody sings.

Mass that persists can always be re-weighed, never only on the original owner's scale.

The gold left long ago and the streets still bend around where it was.

Decay is not a noun, and the verb has a subject. Some of it is harvested: the configuration dismantled to liberate the substance, the argument running backwards in a gully for a few hundred rand. The green leans in over the top of the frame without permission. It keeps being worse.

To photograph a ruin is to weigh it in a field the people inside it cannot afford. Say so, or the picture lies. (The photographer would not photograph the people; the refusal was a toll and an omission, both.)

Still.
