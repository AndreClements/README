## Attribution & Ownership

**Multi-directed Partiality (Multidirected Partiality) is a framework developed by Ivan Boszormenyi-Nagy (1920-2007)**, a Hungarian-American psychiatrist and one of the founders of family therapy.

First articulated in *Invisible Loyalties: Reciprocity in Intergenerational Family Therapy* (1973, with Geraldine Spark) and elaborated in *Between Give and Take: A Clinical Guide to Contextual Therapy* (1986, with Barbara Krasner), Multi-directed Partiality emerged from Nagy's integration of psychodynamic, existential, and systemic approaches with a relational ethics grounding.

**Key Works:**
- Boszormenyi-Nagy, I. & Spark, G. (1973). *Invisible Loyalties: Reciprocity in Intergenerational Family Therapy*. New York: Harper & Row.
- Boszormenyi-Nagy, I. & Krasner, B. (1986). *Between Give and Take: A Clinical Guide to Contextual Therapy*. New York: Brunner/Mazel.

**Lineage:**
```
Psychodynamic Theory (Freud, object relations)
    └── Family Systems Theory (1950s-60s)
            └── Contextual Therapy (Boszormenyi-Nagy, 1973)
                    └── Multi-directed Partiality (methodology)
                            └── Relational Ethics (the fourth dimension)
```

## §1. The Core Question

> *"How can we be fair to everyone in a relational system, including those not present, those who came before, and those yet to come?"*

Boszormenyi-Nagy observed that:
- Every relationship has a "ledger" of give-and-take
- Injustices accumulate across generations (destructive entitlement)
- The therapist's task is not neutrality but **accountable partiality to all**
- Trust is built through acknowledgment of legitimate entitlements

This reframes ethics as **relational balance** — not abstract rules, but the concrete give-and-take between persons across time.

---

## §2. The Five Dimensions of Contextual Therapy

Contextual Therapy integrates five dimensions of human reality. Multi-directed Partiality operates across all of them but finds its home in the fourth (Relational Ethics).

| Dimension | Definition | Focus |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| **1. Facts** | Objective circumstances: genetics, health, history, socioeconomic context | "What are the givens?" |
| **2. Individual Psychology** | Intrapsychic dynamics: defences, motivations, cognitions | "What does this person feel/think?" |
| **3. Systemic Interactions** | Patterns, roles, power dynamics, communication structures | "How does this system function?" |
| **4. Relational Ethics** | Ledger of fairness, loyalty, entitlement, legacy, trustworthiness | "What is owed? What has been given?" |
| **5. Ontic** | Existential dimension: being-in-relation, I-Thou encounter | "What does it mean to truly meet?" |

> **The fourth dimension is Nagy's distinctive contribution.** While others addressed facts, psychology, and systems, he insisted that relational ethics — the actual ledger of give-and-take — is an irreducible reality with causal power.

## §3. Core Constructs

### 3.1 The Ledger of Merits and Entitlements

Every relationship has an implicit **ledger** that tracks:
- What has been given
- What has been received
- What is owed
- What is deserved

```
Ledger := Σ(contributions) − Σ(extractions)

Positive balance → Earned entitlement
Negative balance → Accumulated debt
```

**Key insight:** This ledger is not always conscious, but it operates. People act from their sense of what they deserve based on what they've given.

### 3.2 Destructive Entitlement

When a person has been genuinely wronged but cannot address it with the original wrongdoer, they may feel entitled to extract payment from others.

```
Destructive Entitlement := unaddressed_injury → displaced_claim → harm_to_innocent
```

**Example:** A parent who was abused as a child may feel entitled to harsh treatment of their own children — "I had it worse."

**Critical:** This is not pathology but a relational logic. The entitlement is real; the displacement is the problem.

### 3.3 Loyalty

Loyalty is the **invisible thread** that binds people across generations. It can be:

| Type | Definition | Expression |
|------|------------|------------|
| **Vertical loyalty** | To parents, ancestors, origins | "I owe my family" |
| **Horizontal loyalty** | To partners, peers, community | "I owe my people" |
| **Split loyalty** | Conflict between incompatible loyalties | "I can't please both" |

**Split loyalty** is one of the most painful human experiences — and one of the most common sources of presenting problems.

### 3.4 Legacy

What is passed down — not just material inheritance, but patterns, debts, mandates, and gifts.

```
Legacy := {material, relational, ethical, existential}
```

Each person inherits legacies and creates legacies. Multi-partiality asks: "What legacy is being passed on? What ledger entries are being transferred?"

### 3.5 Trustworthiness

The currency of relational ethics. Built through:
- Acknowledgment of the other's entitlement
- Fair accounting in the ledger
- Willingness to give due credit
- Acceptance of one's own debts

```
Trust := f(acknowledgment, fair_accounting, give_credit, accept_debt)
```

## §4. Multi-directed Partiality as Method

### The Core Stance

Multi-directed Partiality is **not** neutrality. It is the therapist's active stance of:

1. **Acknowledging** the legitimate needs and entitlements of each person
2. **Advocating** for each person's perspective, even those not present
3. **Crediting** contributions that have been made
4. **Balancing** by attending to those most vulnerable at any moment

> *"The therapist strives to be fair to everyone involved."*

### The Movement

```
Person A's perspective → acknowledgment → credit
     ↓
Person B's perspective → acknowledgment → credit
     ↓
Absent person (child, ancestor) → acknowledgment → credit
     ↓
Integration → what emerges from holding all?
```

**Key:** The therapist does not synthesise into a single truth. They hold multiple truths simultaneously, allowing the system to find its own integration.

### Lending Weight

At any moment, some parties need more advocacy than others:
- Children typically need more weight (less power)
- Absent parties need representation
- Those whose suffering led to destructive entitlement need acknowledgment

```
Weight allocation := f(vulnerability, voice_access, historical_debt)
```

## §5. The Give-Take Balance

The fundamental dynamic of relational life:

```
Health := balanced give-take over time
Pathology := persistent imbalance
```

| Imbalance Type | Pattern | Consequence |
|----------------|---------|-------------|
| **Over-giving** | Always give, never receive | Depletion, resentment |
| **Over-taking** | Always receive, never give | Guilt, isolation |
| **Parentification** | Child gives to parent | Developmental disruption |
| **Exploitation** | Systematic extraction | Destructive entitlement |

### Rejunction

The process of rebalancing a relationship through:
1. Acknowledgment of what has been given
2. Recognition of what is owed
3. Negotiation of future give-take
4. Ritual marking of the transition

**Rejunction** is the goal — not blame, not catharsis, but the restoration of trustworthy relating.

---

## §6. The And-Yet (Shadow)

- **Relational focus.** May underweight individual pathology when present
- **Western lineage.** Concepts like "ledger" may not translate across all cultures
- **Complexity.** Holding multiple perspectives requires high cognitive/emotional load
- **Time.** Rejunction is slow; not suited for acute crisis intervention

### Relational Ethics via CARDS

| CARD | Relational Ethics Expression |
|:----:|:-----------------------------|

| **A** | Autonomy to choose one's loyalty commitments; not forced devotion |
| **R** | Relatedness = the matrix where ledgers exist; belonging + obligation |
| **D** | Dignity = acknowledgment of one's entitlements; being credited fairly |

### Destructive Entitlement as CARDS Cascade

```
Injury → D in red (dignity violated)
    → R in red (trust broken)
        → displaced claim on others
            → their D/R/S in red
                → intergenerational cascade
```

**Multi-partiality interrupts this cascade** by acknowledging the original injury (crediting the entitlement) while preventing displacement (protecting others' CARDS).

### The CARDS Ledger

Each CARD can be tracked as a give-take balance:

```
C_ledger := "Have I been helped to grow? Have I helped others?"
A_ledger := "Has my autonomy been respected? Have I respected others'?"
R_ledger := "Have I received care? Have I given care?"
D_ledger := "Have I been seen and credited? Have I seen and credited others?"
```

## Execution Routines

### Routine A: Self-Check (Before Engaging)

1. **Map the stakeholders.** Who is affected? Who is present? Who is absent?
2. **Sense the ledgers.** What has each party given? What do they feel entitled to?
3. **Check for displacement.** Is anyone claiming from the wrong party?
4. **Weight the vulnerable.** Whose voice is least heard?

### Routine B: In Dialogue

1. **Credit before challenging.** Acknowledge what each party has contributed.
2. **Name the loyalty binds.** "You seem caught between..."
3. **Represent the absent.** "What might [absent person] say they need?"
4. **Seek rejunction.** "What would need to happen for balance to be restored?"

### Routine C: System Design

1. **Build acknowledgment into protocols.** Credit contributions explicitly.
2. **Design for multi-voice.** Don't collapse to single perspective.
3. **Preserve dissent.** Log minority views; they may carry truths.
4. **Enable clean exits.** Ledgers can be acknowledged even if unsettled.
