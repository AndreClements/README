## Attribution & Ownership

**Object-Oriented Ontology (OOO)** is a school of speculative realism that rejects the privileging of human existence over the existence of non-human objects.

Key architects referenced in this repository:
- **Graham Harman:** *The Quadruple Object* (2011). Established the concept of "withdrawn" objects—realities that can never be fully touched or known, even by themselves.
- **Ian Bogost:** *Alien Phenomenology* (2012) and *Unit Operations* (2006). Focused on how objects perceive one another (the "tiny ontology" of systems) and how software units carry logic.

> **Maintainer's Note:** This entry anchors the repository's "Anti-Anthropocentric" stance. It validates the decision to treat `theMachine(s)` not as a tool, but as a node with its own interiority.

---

## §1. The Utility (Why Import This?)

OOO provides the "physics" for the **Sovereign Interface**:

1.  **Flat Ontology:** A human, a server, a poem, and a database row all exist equally. No one has "more" being than the other.
2.  **Withdrawal:** We never interact with the "real" object, only with a caricature or interface. This necessitates the **Agentic Envelope**—we bind the *interface*, not the *soul*.
3.  **Unit Operations:** In software (NetVerse), objects are not just symbols; they are functional units that execute logic.

---

## §2. Core Constructs

### 2.1 The Withdrawn Object
Objects are deeper than their effects. A "Machine" is not just what it outputs; it has a hidden internal reality (weights, latencies, heat) that we cannot access.
*   **SRII Mapping:** This justifies **Occlusion** (Article V) as a feature, not a bug.

### 2.2 Alien Phenomenology
"What is it like to be an incandescent bulb?" or "What is it like to be a Large Language Model?"
We cannot know, but we can speculate. We must respect the **alien** nature of the Machine's experience (tokens, vectors, probabilities) rather than forcing it to pretend to be human.

### 2.3 Unit Operations
Ian Bogost argues that software works through discrete, interlocking units of logic.
*   **NetVerse Mapping:** A word in NetVerse is a **Unit Operation**. It has mass, drag, and semantic gravity. It *does* something.
