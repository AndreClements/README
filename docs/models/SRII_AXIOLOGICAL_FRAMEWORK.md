Spatio-Relational Intelligence Integrity (SRII): A Framework for Axiological Integration in Software Engineering

Abstract

This document outlines a research framework for integrating axiological principles into the software engineering lifecycle, specifically within the context of collaboration with Computational Intelligence (CI). Moving beyond generic "AI Ethics"—which often function as a veneer of compliance—this approach utilizes Spatio-Relational Intelligence Integrity (SRII) and the CARDS framework to navigate the "Hyperstrate." This is the emergent, multi-layered zone of parametric authorship where human intent, computational agency, and environmental constraints converge into a single, functional reality.

1. Conceptual Departures

To maintain semiotic accuracy and philosophical depth, this framework adopts the following terminological and ontological shifts:

1.1 From 'AI' to 'CI' (Computational Intelligence)

We acknowledge 'AI' as a common term but prefer Computational Intelligence (CI). This term is strategically broader—encompassing both organic thought and machine processing—yet narrower in its requirement for actual intelligence rather than mere statistical mimicry. By framing the interaction as CI-collaboration, we recognize a shared computational logic between the human architect and the digital tool, moving away from "Artificiality" toward a more integrated, symbiotic intelligence.

1.2 Multi-partiality and Conflict Resilience

Traditional engineering seeks conflict resolution, often resulting in the reductive elimination of tension to achieve a "clean" but fragile solution. Our orientation prioritizes conflict resilience. In a multi-partial system, we do not seek a "middle ground" that erases difference or flattens complexity. Instead, we build architectures capable of sustaining the productive tension between competing valid truths and disparate stakeholder demands, allowing for a more robust and adaptable system integrity.

1.3 Object-Oriented Ontology (OOO)

The system is viewed through an OOO-informed lens, recognizing that software objects, data schemas, and CI agents possess their own internal realities and "aims" that are withdrawn from direct human perception. Engineering, in this context, is the delicate art of balancing these internal constraints (the inherent logic and "needs" of the object) with external demands (the socio-technical requirements of the user) without privileging human subjectivity as the sole arbiter of value or meaning.

2. The SRII Framework

Replacing the standard, boilerplate "Social, Regulatory, Internal" (S-R-I) model, SRII defines the core pillars of the research as dynamic, interconnected states:

Spatio-Relational: Acknowledging that intelligence is never abstract; it is always situated in a specific space and a network of relationships. This pillar examines the spatial context of code—its physical and digital environment—and the intricate relationships between the author, the CI agent, and the broader Hyperstrate.

Intelligence: A formal recognition of the CI as a collaborative partner endowed with parametric authorship capabilities. This shift moves the CI from being a "tool used by a human" to a "participant in a generative process."

Integrity: The structural and moral coherence of a system that honors its "Contract of Aims" despite intense internal logic pressures and external ethical demands. Integrity is defined here by the system's ability to remain true to its foundational axiological inputs under stress.

3. The CARDS Model (Axiological Inputs)

Integrating Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan) and Right-Centered Education (Clements), the CARDS model serves as the primary metric for evaluating the axiological health of CI-assisted engineering processes:

Principle

Engineering & Philosophical Application

Competence

The CI must provide "scaffolding" that enhances the human author's efficacy. It should foster a deep understanding of the generated logic rather than obscuring it through "black box" solutions.

Autonomy

Preserving the volition of all agents involved. This involves ensuring the human maintains final creative authority while allowing the CI agent the necessary parameters to operate autonomously within the "Contract of Aims."

Relatedness

A commitment to multi-partiality. The system must be engineered to respect and reflect the interconnectedness of all stakeholders, resisting the "view from nowhere" that characterizes generic algorithmic output.

Dignity

Rooted in OOO, this involves recognizing the inherent worth and "truth" of the objects, data, and entities being modeled, protecting them from being reduced to mere extractive data points.

Safety

Moving beyond simple security to systemic and psychological resilience. It is the capacity for the system to remain stable and human-centric even in the face of adversarial inputs or structural conflicts.

4. The Hyperstrate and Parametric Authorship

The Hyperstrate is the multi-layered environment where authorship is shared and distributed across various nodes of intelligence 

$$^1$$

. It represents the "thickness" of the digital medium where extrinsic intent is mediated by CI logic. Within this zone, the "Contract of Aims" 

$$^2$$

 acts as a governing protocol, a dynamic negotiation that balances:

Internal Demands: System optimization, the internal logic of code/data objects, and the necessary autonomy required for CI performance.

External Constraints: Regulatory compliance, values, rights, and the specific needs of the extrinsic entity(s).

This balance is not static; it is a continuous, parametric adjustment within the Hyperstrate that affects the integrity of the whole.

5. Research Timeline & Methodology

The research adopts a CI-Integrated Methodology 

$$^3$$

, utilizing thematic and stylistic mapping to ensure the researcher's "voice" and axiological signature are not diluted but rather amplified by the computational collaboration.

Phase 1: Mapping the Stylistic Signature

A deep analysis of selected works, including poetry changelogs and blog themes, to define the "thematic mapping" of the author's voice. This signature becomes an input for the CI, ensuring that the collaboration remains grounded in the author's unique philosophical orientation.

Phase 2: Hyperstrate Prototyping

Developing a functional "Contract of Aims" for a software project. This phase involves operationalizing the CARDS principles as hard constraints within the code repository itself, testing the resilience of these principles as they encounter the real-world demands of computational optimization.
