**Lead Maintainer's Note.** This model names something that was operating before it was named. Sketchbook pages answer each other across the gutter without being planned to. Sometimes the pairing is noticed after the fact ("the accident of sequential pages"). Sometimes it runs below awareness entirely (the maker turns past both pages without registering the exchange). Either way, it operates. The model treats that operation as load-bearing, arguably. It is the first of a possible set of span-models; it may extend to cover other kinds of gutter (repo-to-repo, show-to-show, decade-to-decade).

Host ≠ Author still applies: the author is the practice; the host is this repo.

## Axiom: The Gutter is a Boundary with usually at least Four Valences.

A **span** is a pairing across a structural break — a gutter, a fold, a boundary between two things that are nominally separate. The sketchbook spread is the canonical case (two pages hinged at a gutter), but the move scales: spread → sketchbook → book-of-work → repo → repo-constellation. The gutter is wherever two surfaces meet.

A boundary is not one thing. Usually at least four valences[^tetra] operate:

1. **Skin.** Containment, sovereignty, the bounded arena. The gutter separates two pages; it is what keeps them distinct. (The arena essay's "bounded by skin, by mortality" lives here.)
2. **Sensor.** Reception, afferent channel. The gutter registers what the practice deposited on either side of it. It is what *reads* the adjacency.
3. **Cinema screen.** Projection surface. The gutter is where accumulated attention is displayed — the spread as an image encountered by a viewer (including the maker returning to the page).
4. **Computer screen.** Interface, I/O. The gutter mediates between substrates: hand and archive, page and page, repo and repo.

Each span is a moment where one valence dominates or they cohabit. The same fold is skin, sensor, screen, interface — differently, at different times, sometimes at once. The count is open: a fifth valence (membrane? archive? tympanum?) may surface in use, and the model does not close the list.

[^tetra]: *Plain-English (the tetravalence rider):* Carbon is tetravalent. So are methane and silicon. If your spine has been accumulating charcoal dust — and the life-drawing practice guarantees this B-), empirically, literally — then you have a tetravalent spine, philosophy entirely aside. The four-valence boundary is not purely a figure of speech: carbon's bonding grammar -- that it is "tetravalent" -- is what lets the charcoal mark be held by the paper in the first place. Also mark-making rides on chemistry it did not invent. The model is just noticing. B^)

                    ── ZONE 1: THE CONCEPT ──

## §1. The Gutter

A gutter is any boundary between two surfaces that could be read together. The sketchbook's binding is the native case. But a gutter also appears between the live-drawing session and the photographic archive of it if it has such, between one repo and its sibling, between the work of 2017 and the work of 2026. Wherever two things are hinged closely enough to listen to each other, a gutter is operating.

The move of the model(/sitter) is to take the gutter seriously as a site — not a mere separation, but a surface that has its own behaviour. The four valences above are the grammar of that behaviour.

## §2. Four Registers of Span

A span is instantiated in one of four registers. The registers are not mutually exclusive; a single pairing may drift between them.

**Noticed span.** The pairing recognised after the fact. "The pairing was not planned (it is the accident of sequential pages in a working sketchbook). And yet." (Arena essay, on Gustav/Andrea.) The maker notices the exchange and registers it. Noticing is retroactive, arguably always.

**Unnoticed span.** The pairing that operates without ever being surfaced. A page asks; a later page answers; the maker turns past both without registering the exchange. The dialogue runs under awareness. This register is the model's hinge claim — that attention is not a precondition nor an anti-condition of integrity. The sketchbook metabolises its own contents. So do artists. So do we, to some more-or-less degree, all. 

**Solicited span.** The archive searched for an answer to a live-session question. "The session yields a gesture; the archive answers it." (Arena essay, on the cruciform-and-archive-hand.) Here the maker names the question and solicits the pairing, but without pre-specifying the form of the answer.

**Cross-book span.** The pairing that runs between projects, books, or repos. The plan-and-output pair between README and ArtPraxis is a cross-book span. The relationship between a painting and a codebase is another. The gutter stretches across substrates.

## §3. Accumulation Without Prediction

Spans are continuous with layered-averaging. In `bodyTime(fugitivity)`, two moments of a figure are printed onto a single surface; the composite is a form neither moment predicted. In `layer-average.py`, 24 images are stacked and yield an emergent figure. In manual sketchbook averaging, the hand accumulates instances of a gesture to find the form underneath.

A span is the same move across a different axis. Instead of accumulating on a single surface, accumulation runs across adjacent surfaces hinged at a gutter. The authorial gaze is loose enough to let the pairing compose itself. Form emerges when the gaze is loose, arguably — when selection does not pre-specify the outcome.

This is the **Constraint Gift** in span form: the gutter as a generative constraint. The fold does not obstruct the dialogue; it enables it. The pages listen better because they are bound.

## §4. The And-Yet (Shadow)

The risk is pareidolia. Any two adjacent pages can be read as a pairing if the reader is primed to find one. The model must not license retroactive over-reading. Every claimed span is a candidate span until tested.

The Sceptic's test (per [observerCircuitBreaker_DBC_CQS.md](../protocols/observerCircuitBreaker_DBC_CQS.md)): Eg. *Would another reader, not told to look for a pairing, register a resonance independently?* If not, the span is in the reader's head, not neccesarily in the spread.

A second shadow: the unnoticed-span register is unfalsifiable by construction. A dialogue that "runs below awareness" cannot be directly audited. The model compensates by requiring that claimed unnoticed spans surface *eventually* — the maker must be able, at some later moment, to say "yes, that was happening, I see it now." If no such moment ever arrives, the claim collapses to pareidolia.

A third: naming the span may contaminate the practice. A sketchbook sweep run with span-detection in mind may cease to produce unnoticed spans (because attention has tightened). The execution routines below address this with a *surfacing-without-contaminating* discipline.

A fourth, meta: this model, applied to itself, requires that spans.md and `spans_field_essay.md` pair across their own gutter. If the two documents do not operate as a cross-book span, the model's claim is performative rather than demonstrated.

## §6. Integration with CARDS

| CARDS axis | Span-side implication | Diagnostic |
|---|---|---|
| **Competence** | Noticing-without-contaminating is a skill. A maker who can surface unnoticed spans without collapsing the register is practising span-competence. | If the sweep protocol consistently produces only noticed spans, competence has drifted — attention has tightened. |
| **Autonomy** | The page's right to answer on its own terms. A solicited span honours autonomy when the soliciting gesture does not pre-specify the form of the answer. | If the archive answers exactly what was asked, autonomy is at risk — the maker has leaked the answer into the question. |
| **Relatedness** | Pages as interlocutors. The gutter is a site of significant relation; the span is what two pages say to each other. | If pairings never emerge across a working sketchbook, relatedness is low — the pages are not in conversation. |
| **Dignity** | Not forcing resolution. A span may remain unresolved; the model does not require closure. "And yet" is the span's native grammar. | If every claimed span is reduced to a tidy reading, dignity is at risk. |
| **Safety** | The unnoticed-span register is a trust exercise with one's own practice. The maker has to trust that the sketchbook is metabolising content faithfully even when not watched. | If the maker cannot trust unwatched accumulation, safety is compromised; the practice becomes surveillance. |

**Diagnostic implication (bidirectional)**: CARDS degradation along any axis suggests a specific span-failure. If Relatedness drops, check whether the pages are being allowed to listen to each other (or whether each is being made to perform alone). If Dignity drops, check whether the maker is forcing pairings into closure.

## §7. Integration with Landscapification and Philosophy of Body

**Landscapification**: spans are topology. The gutter is a contour line. A sketchbook sweep is a field-reading. (See [docs/essays/landscapification_lineage_field_essay.md](../essays/landscapification_lineage_field_essay.md).) Where Landscapification maps internal states onto spatial fields, spans map temporal dialogue onto structural adjacency. The two models are near-siblings.

**Philosophy of Body** ([docs/models/philosophy_of_body.md](philosophy_of_body.md)): the body is the first and final arena; it is "where a map is forced to become a territory, a territory a map." The span model is continuous with §3 (*The Body as Sovereign Boundary*, the firewall-before-port logic). A span is what happens when sovereignty is maintained on either side of the gutter *and* the port is opened anyway. The boundary-as-skin is the body; the same boundary as sensor/screen/interface is the span.

Strain point: Philosophy of Body locates synthesis *in* the body. Spans locate a different kind of synthesis *across* a gutter that is not the body (the sketchbook spread, the repo boundary). The strain is productive: it clarifies that the arena is not only the single body but any bounded surface hinged to another.

## §8. Execution Routines

**Routine A — Sketchbook sweep (surfacing-without-contaminating).**
Return to a working sketchbook at least one session after it was last worked. Read it through without pausing to analyse. Mark spreads that *arrest* you (no explanation required). Only after the full sweep, return to the marked spreads and ask: what pairing is operating here? The delay somewhat protects the unnoticed register from being collapsed by the act of looking.

**Routine B — Cross-repo span check (quarterly).**
For each active repo (README, ArtPraxis, PRIVATE_META, site), note what question that repo asked this quarter and what answer arrived from a sibling. Log cross-book spans to `staging/reflections/`. (This plan is itself an instance.)

**Routine C — Exhibition design application.**
When sequencing works for a show, treat the sequence as a sketchbook. Let adjacent works pair without forcing narrative resolution. The Gustav/Andrea pairing in the arena essay is the worked example: two registers, one gutter, no insistence.

**Routine D — Archive-as-answer.**
Name a question a live session is asking (on a facing page, in a margin, in a note). Search the archive for a work that answers it. Place the found work as the second half of the spread. The question pre-exists; the answer is sourced after. (Solicited span register.)

**Routine E — Sceptic pass for claimed spans.**
For each claimed span, ask: *Would another reader, uninformed of my claim, register this resonance independently?* If yes, provisional pass. If no, log as candidate-pareidolia and considder setting aside unjustified against wider context (Yea, good luck with calculating that, I know.).
