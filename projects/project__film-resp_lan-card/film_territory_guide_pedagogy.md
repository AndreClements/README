## 1. Rationale and Purpose
The "Filmic Response" project presents students with a list of over 50 films. For the target cohort, this degree of choice can induce paralysis and disengagement, acting as a significant barrier to entry.

The purpose of this intervention is to `Landscapify()` this list—to transform it from a flat, intimidating list into a conceptual map of navigable "territories." This provides a crucial layer of **orientation** for the students, allowing them to make a more autonomous and personally meaningful choice. This directly serves the core pedagogical goals of **Safety** (by reducing cognitive overload), **Dignity** (by respecting their time and attention), and **Autonomy** (by scaffolding their decision-making process).

## 2. The Underlying Model: The Enneagram as Armature
To ensure the clusters are conceptually robust, the nine territories are structured around the **Enneagram model of personality**. This model was chosen over traditional genre classifications because it maps **underlying motivations, fears, and worldviews**, rather than superficial plot elements. This allows us to group films by their thematic and stylistic "DNA," providing a much deeper and more artistically useful set of connections.

The Enneagram framework itself is purely an internal armature for our pedagogical design and **is not intended to be exposed to the students**. They will only interact with the archetypal names and descriptions. Alternative topologies like the so-called Big-Five or Big-6 could also work but the Enneagram topology is well suited to our requirements in this context in the oppinion of this author.

The mapping is as follows:

| Territory Name | Enneagram Type | Core Dynamic / Worldview |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. The Grid | Type 1: The Reformer | World as a system to be perfected; focus on order, integrity, and critique. |
| 2. The Empath's Mirror | Type 2: The Helper | World as a web of relationships; focus on connection, service, and intimacy. |
| 3. In the Spotlight | Type 3: The Achiever | World as a stage for performance; focus on image, success, and authenticity. |
| 4. A Dreamer's Loss | Type 4: The Individualist | World as a symbolic landscape; focus on identity, melancholy, and beauty. |
| 5. Hideout | Type 5: The Investigator | World as a puzzle to be understood; focus on knowledge, observation, and detachment.|
| 6. Watchtower | Type 6: The Loyalist | World as an uncertain and potentially hostile place; focus on safety, trust, and belief.|
| 7. Wanderer's Carnival | Type 7: The Enthusiast | World as an adventure to be experienced; focus on freedom, escape, and spectacle. |
| 8. Rebel's Fortress | Type 8: The Challenger | World as an arena for power; focus on justice, control, and direct confrontation. |
| 9. The Silent River | Type 9: The Peacemaker | World as a space for harmony; focus on atmosphere, presence, and connection. |

## 3. Connection to Broader Pedagogical Framework
This `Landscapify()` intervention is a direct application of the core principles outlined in the main pedagogical framework:

*   **Differential Sophistication:** This map is a primary tool of differential sophistication. It provides a simple, metaphorical entry point (the nine territory descriptions) that allows students of all confidence levels to access a complex set of choices. It meets them where they are, without simplifying the available options.
*   **Parametric Authorship:** By choosing a territory, the student is making their first, high-level "parametric" choice. They are selecting a conceptual world and a set of thematic constraints that will inform the more specific rules they later define on their "Language Card."
*   **Self-Determination Theory (SDT):** The map directly supports **Autonomy** by making the act of choosing a film less overwhelming and more personally meaningful. It builds **Competence** by giving students a framework to understand and articulate *why* they chose a particular film.
