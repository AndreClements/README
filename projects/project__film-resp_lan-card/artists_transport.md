**(Note: This script is designed to be spoken in a relaxed, conversational tone. The pauses are important to let ideas land and settle.)**

### The Introduction

"Greetings human students. Let's start with a few questions. Just think about it for a moment, please...

How did you get here today? Did you walk? Catch a taxi, a bus, an Uber, a private jet? Maybe you got a ride with someone?

*(Pause)*

Okay. So we all use some kind of **transport** to get around.

Now, think about the kind of transport you *want*. What kind of vehicle it involves. Is a train going exactly where you need to go, or just where the tracks are? In an Uber, who is really in control of the journey? What would it feel like to have your own set of wheels, that takes you exactly where you want to go, the way you want, whenever you want?

*(Pause)*

I'm asking you this because this project is about exactly that. It's about your **artistic transport** - there is a sense in which an artist's signature, specifically what that signature represents - is their vehicle. During this project you get to work on the stuff you as artist will sign, but you also get to work on what that signature might mean, what value it will have. Your signature - and not just the obvious visible version of it but what it means in terms of your style and values, is a kind of vehicle, transport, for your future career. 

Your unique, personal style, the way you make art, is the vehicle that will carry you through your career. This project is your chance to get into the workshop and start designing your own.

### The Main Tool: Your Language Card

The main tool we will use to do this is your **Language Card**. It is the blueprint for your own artistic vehicle.

It has three simple rules that **you** will decide on:
1.  **Edge:** Your rule for how lines and shapes meet each other, or disappear.
2.  **Value:** Your plan for using light and dark.
3.  **Rhythm:** Your rule for the patterns and marks that you repeat in the drawing.

### The Process: Why We Work This Way

You will choose a film from the list, not to copy it, but to get ideas for your rules.

The brief asks you to make five practice drawings first. Why? Because, as the proverb says, **'a single bracelet does not jingle.'** This means one idea alone is too quiet; we need to see you test several ideas to find a strong one.

The final A0 drawing is a very big job. It can feel like trying to eat an elephant. But the proverb tells us, **'the best way to eat an elephant is to cut him up into pieces.'** Your Language Card is how you cut the job into small, manageable pieces. You just focus on following your own rules, one at a time.

And remember, we don't want a copy of a scene from the film. Anyone can take a picture of a roaring lion. But as the saying goes, **'a roaring lion kills no game.'** This means a picture of the lion doesn't show its real power. We want to see the *idea* behind the film, the feeling, the energy, the spirit.

### The Task: What We Are Doing Today

So, what are we doing right now? Three simple things:

1.  **Choose your film** and write **one simple sentence** about its feeling or style (no plot).
2.  Use that sentence to draft the **first version of your three rules** on your Language Card.
3.  Make your **first A4 test drawing** to see how your rules work.

### The Main Idea to Remember

If you forget everything else, hold this in your head. This is the core of the project, EVR:

**Edge holds. Value locks. Rhythm carries.**

Okay. Let's get into the workshop.
