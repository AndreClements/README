# Teaching Framework: A New Approach for the "Filmic Response" Project

## 1. Introduction
This document explains a new teaching approach for the Learning Unit Six "Filmic Response" project, as detailed in the **Drawing II (BADR7Y2) Learning Guide – 2024**. It is designed for second-year students who sometimes struggle with motivation or who may be worried about the cost of materials.

This plan gives more support for the existing project brief, which is conceptually difficult. The goal is to help students engage more deeply, feel more in control of their work, and begin to develop their own unique artistic style, in line with the guide's aim to "develop a confident body of drawings in preparation for your 3rd year" (UJ, 2024:2). The plan includes a short workshop using AI for ideas, a tool called a "Language Card," and a final self-reflection exercise.

## 2. Main Challenges of the Project
*   **The Challenge of 'Metaphor':** The brief asks for a "metaphor," not a simple illustration. This is a difficult idea that can stop students before they even start.
*   **The Challenge of Working Alone:** Students have to do a lot of work over the vacation. Without structure, they can get lost or leave it all to the last minute.
*   **The Challenge of the Big, Blank Page:** A large A0 drawing is scary. Students need a strong, clear idea they believe in before they can begin.
*   **The Challenge of Using Simple Materials:** Students with limited budgets can fall back on clichés. We want to help them use cheap materials in creative and exciting ways.

## 3. The New Approach: "The Metaphor Lab & Production Brief"
We will structure the project like a professional design studio. This gives students a clear, step-by-step process.

*   **Phase 1: The Metaphor Lab:** A workshop before the vacation. Here, we use AI for a short time to help students think of metaphors. This helps them make a clear plan for their first 5 practice drawings.
*   **Phase 2: The 'Greenlight' Pitch:** At the start of term, students present their practice drawings to get their final idea "greenlit" or approved. After this, they create their "Language Card" which contains the style rules for their final piece.
*   **Phase 3: Production:** Students make their final A0 drawing. They will follow the rules on their Language Card. They also have the choice to add an "Obstruction" (an extra challenge) to push their skills further.

## 4. Key Teaching Tools and Why We Use Them

### The AI "Metaphor Engine"
> **Why we use it:** This tool helps students feel more capable and confident. It quickly gives them many different visual ideas, which reduces the fear of starting. The student is in control because they choose which ideas to keep and develop. The AI is just a tool to start their thinking.

### The "Language Card v0.1"
> **Why we use it:** This is the most important tool for helping students find and describe their own artistic style. It asks them to write down simple rules for how they use lines, light and dark, and marks. This makes their artistic style something clear and real that they can use again in future projects. To make this tangible for students, we frame this as the 'blueprint for their artistic vehicle'—connecting the abstract idea of a 'personal voice' to the practical concept of a career vehicle they can build and own.

### The Self-Assessment "Growth Audit"
> **Why we use it:** This tool helps students think about their own learning process. By asking them to give points to different parts of the project, we encourage them to see value in the whole journey – including the difficult parts. This respects their effort and helps them focus on growth, not just the final grade.

## 5. How This Connects to Learning Theory (SDT)
This approach operationalizes key concepts from the Learning Guide, such as fostering "disruption and rupture" in a positive light (UJ, 2024:8) and avoiding the "impoverishment" of "thin and predictable ideas" (UJ, 2024:7). The framework is designed through the lens of **Differential Sophistication** (Clements, 2023b), providing multiple access points for students with varying levels of confidence and skill.

This new approach is based on Self-Determination Theory (SDT), which says that students are most motivated when three needs are met:

*   **Autonomy** (the feeling of choice and control): Students choose their film, their main idea, and the rules for their own Language Card.
*   **Competence** (the feeling of being good at something): The workshop gives them ideas to start with, and the 'greenlight' pitch confirms they are on the right track.
*   **Relatedness** (the feeling of connecting with others): Students share ideas and give feedback during the workshop and the pitch session.

We also build on a foundation of **Safety and Dignity**, where it is okay to try new things and where every student's personal response is respected.

## 6. What We Hope to See (Expected Outcomes)
*   Better quality and more thoughtful final drawings.
*   Fewer drawings that just copy a scene from the film.
*   Students who can talk more confidently about their own work and their artistic choices.
*   A clear starting point (the Language Card) that students can use in their next project.
*   A change in focus from "What grade will I get?" to "What did I learn on this project?"
