## Core argument

In the first half of the third Critique, Kant argues that a pure judgement of taste is disinterested and rests not on the concept or purpose of an object but on the "form of purposiveness" — an object's appearing as if designed for our cognitive faculties while serving no determinate end (purposiveness without a purpose), which sets the free play of imagination and understanding into harmony. He distinguishes free beauty (*pulchritudo vaga*), which presupposes no concept of what the thing ought to be, from dependent or adherent beauty (*pulchritudo adhaerens*), which is judged against a concept of the object's perfection or function. Judgements of taste claim universal subjective validity without resting on proof.

## What to internalise — in your voice

Kant gives me the cleanest old name for what A3★ is reaching toward: felt beauty as the registration of form that looks chosen, fitted, purposive — and yet serves no fixed end. Purposiveness without a purpose. The pleasure tracks the legibility of the constraint, less the constraint's product. That is almost exactly the claim I want to make about a parametric weave: the beauty is in the fact that a rule held, freely, and shows itself holding.

And yet there is a seam I should not smooth. Kant splits free beauty from dependent beauty: the second is judged against a concept of what the thing ought to be, its perfection, its function. My 1/N hinge — equal weight, no source crowned — is a dignity-conservation rule, which makes the averaged composite a dependent beauty: it answers to a concept (consent, no crowding). Kant would say that constrains the taste-judgement, taints its purity. I read it the other way: the constraint is the gift, the floor the form. The dignity gate is the concept the beauty adheres to, on purpose.

The terrain to walk carefully: a man, averaging thirty-five historical Eves, theorising the gaze. Kant's disinterested judge is suspiciously bodiless, suspiciously universal — a "common sense" that presumes everyone stands where he stands. Borrow the structure of purposiveness. Distrust the universality. The body keeps its veto.
