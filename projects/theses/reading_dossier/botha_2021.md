# Botha 2021 — 'Risk'

**Citation.** Botha, M. (2021) 'Risk', in Waugh, P. and Botha, M. (eds) *Future Theory: A Handbook to Critical Concepts.* London: Bloomsbury Academic, pp. 411–432. **[Gold]**

**Access.** In copyright — author-posted preprint: <https://www.academia.edu/82721202/Risk>. (Confirm against the published chapter before quoting pages.)

## Core argument

Botha tracks a rift in how risk is thought: an abstract, future-facing calculus of probability (Beck's risk society, actuarial and economic modelling) set against a visceral, located experience of risk borne unevenly by specific, vulnerable bodies. Drawing on Beck, Butler and Mbembe, he argues that risk is never neutral but distributed through relations of power, so that the agency of risk-taking and the exposure of risk-being-imposed cannot be separated. The chapter holds that vulnerability is not the opposite of agency but its condition: to act on the future at all is to be exposed, and the ethical question is who carries the exposure others choose.

## What to internalise — in your voice

The hinge I keep returning to: risk taken and risk imposed are not two sizes of the same thing. One is chosen exposure, the body putting itself forward. The other is exposure routed onto someone who never agreed to carry it, and at that point it stops being risk and becomes harm. Botha keeps these close together, refuses to let the calculus (probability, the actuarial future) wash the located body out of the picture. That refusal is what I want in the paper.

For Parametric Authorship this lands on the dignity gate, and on the gender tension I keep circling. A man averaging 35 historical Eves, theorising consent and the gaze: the risk of that gesture is mine to take, and yet the exposure may settle on bodies that did not consent to being averaged. The 1/N hinge reads differently here. Equal weight is not innocence. Distributing attention evenly across N sources can still externalise a cost onto a body that the calculus cannot see.

The harder thing, and the one I should not resolve: Botha says agency itself requires vulnerability. To make anything that touches the future is to be exposed. So the gate cannot be the elimination of risk. Only the test of who carries it, freely or not.
