# Langton 2009 — *Sexual Solipsism*

**Citation.** Langton, R. (2009) *Sexual Solipsism: Philosophical Essays on Pornography and Objectification.* Oxford: Oxford University Press. 405 pp. ISBN 9780199247066. **[Gold]**

**Access.** In copyright — no legitimate full text. Google Books preview: <https://books.google.com/books/about/Sexual_Solipsism.html?id=N-ITDAAAQBAJ>. (Substance carried by the summary + note below.)

## Core argument

Langton extends Nussbaum's seven-feature cluster account of objectification by adding three further features — reduction to body, reduction to appearance, and silencing — while locating autonomy-denial and instrumentality at the conceptual core. She argues that objectification is often not merely a matter of treating a person as a thing but a self-fulfilling projection: perceiving and treating women as subordinate helps make them subordinate, so the harm runs through belief and perception, not only conduct. The collection deploys ethics, philosophy of language, and epistemology to show when treating someone instrumentally curdles into a wrong against their autonomy and standing.

## What to internalise — in your voice

Nussbaum gave us a cluster; Langton widens it: reduction to body, reduction to appearance, silencing. Three more features for the taxonomy, and they earn their place by naming what averaging a face actually does. When I composite 35 historical images of Eve I am reducing to appearance by design — that is the method. The question Langton hands me is the dignity gate: at what point does instrumentality curdle. Her answer is not a count of features but a hinge — autonomy-denial. Use someone as a means and you may still be fine; deny their freely-chosen weight, crown one source over the others, and you have crossed. That maps cleanly onto 1/N = dignity-conservation: equal weight is the floor that keeps instrumental use from becoming objectification. And yet the gender tension does not dissolve. A man theorising consent and the gaze while compositing women's faces sits inside the very projection she describes: the perception that helps make the thing it claims to see. I should not resolve this. Langton lets me hold it: the silencing feature is a warning that the apparatus can speak over the sources it averages. A3★ asks the constraint be legible and freely-chosen — her work is the test for when it stops being either.
