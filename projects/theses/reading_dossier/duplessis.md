## Core argument

Against the standard reading of asylum casebook photographs as uniform mug shots — archetypes of classification, control and surveillance — du Plessis argues that three Grahamstown casebook images, diverse in genre, style and origin, accrue meaning beyond any dominant clinical narrative and can render patients as individual subjects. He locates the interpretative weight in the sitters' own acts of posing and self-presentation and their connections to socio-cultural worlds outside the asylum. Methodologically he refuses diagnostic labels and medical terminology, naming the sitters by their actual names rather than case numbers, so as not to add a further layer to their silence. The 2014 *Social Dynamics* piece sets the institutional frame: photographs made for public consumption constructed a respectable moral-therapy image of the asylum.

## What to internalise — in your voice

A clinical archive is terrain that crowns one viewpoint: the institution's gaze flattens hundreds of incarcerated people into near-identical cases, a striking uniformity across all groups. Du Plessis works against that crowning. His method is small and exact: drop the diagnostic labels, restore the sitter's actual name. That is a 1/N move in a historical register — refusing to let the clinical frame carry all the weight, giving the sitter's own act of posing equal footing in how the image reads. The constraint is the gift: by withholding the medical vocabulary he could legitimately deploy, he opens room for the person to appear.

And the tension I cannot put down: these sitters could not consent — to the camera then, to my reading now. A man theorising the gaze on represented bodies that never agreed to be looked at. Du Plessis names it as care (to obscure the name is to add a layer to the silence), and yet care exercised across an unbridgeable consent-gap stays maculate. This is the dignity floor working backwards, on the dead: the question is whether attention can preserve dignity it was never granted permission to touch. For my Eve work this is the near-twin case. Hold it open. Less resolution, more weight.
