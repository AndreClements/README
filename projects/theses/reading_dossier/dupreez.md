## Core argument

Across this body of work du Preez argues against the cyber-utopian fantasy of leaving the body behind: embodiment is not a limit to be transcended by new technologies but the irreducible ground from which gendered, raced, and classed experience is made, and any honest account of digital culture must stay material rather than escape into disembodied transcendence. Her cyberfeminist reading insists that social categories are carried into and reshaped by technological mediation, so the burdens and stakes of "the cyber" fall unevenly. The later *Spaced Out* extends the same critique to the sublime, tracing a visual-cultural history of "spaced out" encounters — flying, falling, orbit, screen — that displace gravity, locality, home, and the lived body.

## What to internalise — in your voice

A local voice, UP, working the exact seam my paper sits on: the body as the thing the technology keeps promising to let us leave, and her refusal of that promise. Worth holding close because she catches the move before it dresses up as neutral — disembodiment is never evenly distributed; some bodies get to "transcend" because others carry the weight. That maps onto the dignity floor directly. A system that averages away the body, the way an averaged Eve dissolves thirty-five women into one composite, is performing exactly the transcendence she distrusts: gender as a variable to be smoothed rather than a constraint to be honoured. And the gender tension is mine to sit in, not resolve: a man theorising consent and the gaze while doing the averaging. Du Preez does not let me off that hook, and I should not want her to. The sublime turn matters too — felt beauty (A3★) as something that can lift you out of locality, gravity, home. The question her work plants: does the lift track a freely-chosen, legible, dignity-preserving constraint, or does it just feel like flight because someone below is bearing the cost. En tog: embodiment as the constraint that gives the work its rigour, less escape, more ground.
