## Carse's Final Sentence & the Collapse of Evil

*From correspondence with Je'anna L Clements, 2026-02-10.*

"There is but one infinite game." — The entire final chapter of Carse's book is this single sentence. If there is but one infinite game, then everything is already within it. One never treats a game of chess as infinite, but one can play it in infinite mode. One can play anything in infinite mode. And anything in finite mode.

Carse's definition of evil — finite play forcibly imposed on infinite domains — collapses under his own final sentence. If everything is already within the one infinite game, evil is not a game-type problem but a *mode* problem. He knows it too, but doesn't understand how his own contradictions arise.

**Thread:** The binary (finite/infinite) is scaffolding the author dismantles in his own closing move. What remains is not two kinds of game but two modes of play within the one game. This may be Je'anna's deepest contribution to the Carse reading — and it connects directly to CARDS as a mode-diagnostic, not a game-diagnostic.
