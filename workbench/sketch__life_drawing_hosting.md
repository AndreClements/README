## Core Insight: Drama Triangle in the Life Room

Life drawing sessions naturally generate Drama Triangle dynamics:

### The Roles (Unhealthy Expression)

| Role | Position | Drama Expression |
|------|----------|------------------|
| **Model** | Exposed, held, witnessed | Victim: "I'm being judged, objectified, used" |
| **Drawer** | Observing, capturing, interpreting | Persecutor: "I'm capturing you, judging your form" / Rescuer: "I'll make you beautiful" |
| **Facilitator** | Holding space, directing, timing | Rescuer: "I'll make it safe for everyone" / Persecutor: "You must pose like this" |

### The Switches

Drama intensifies through role-switching:
- Model feels Victim → expresses discomfort → becomes Persecutor toward drawers
- Drawer feels Rescuer → creates flattering image → feels unappreciated → becomes Victim
- Facilitator feels Rescuer → overworks to maintain safety → burns out → becomes Victim

### The Payoffs (Why Drama Persists)

| Role | Payoff |
|------|--------|
| Model as Victim | Avoids owning their agency in choosing to be seen |
| Drawer as Persecutor | Maintains distance; treats model as object, not subject |
| Facilitator as Rescuer | Feels essential; avoids own vulnerability |

## Exit: Winner's Triangle in the Life Room

Clean Language + hosting methodology transforms the dynamics:

### The Roles (Healthy Expression)

| Drama Role | Winner's Role | Life Drawing Expression | Clean Question |
|------------|---------------|------------------------|----------------|
| Model as Victim | **Vulnerable** | "I'm choosing to be seen; I have agency in how I'm held" | "And what would you like to have happen?" |
| Drawer as Persecutor | **Assertive** | "I'm witnessing, not judging; my marks are my response, not your truth" | "And that's like what?" |
| Drawer as Rescuer | **Caring** | "I'm responding to what's there, not 'fixing' or 'beautifying'" | "And is there anything else about...?" |
| Facilitator as Rescuer | **Caring/Coaching** | "I'm holding conditions, not saving anyone" | "And what needs to happen for [X]?" |

### The Empowerment Dynamic (TED)

| Drama Role | TED Role | Life Drawing Expression |
|------------|----------|------------------------|
| Model as Victim | **Creator** | "I'm creating the conditions for witnessing through my presence" |
| Drawer as Persecutor | **Challenger** | "I'm offering a response that might reveal something" |
| Facilitator as Rescuer | **Coach** | "I'm supporting capacity, not dependency" |

## Clean Language in the Life Room

### Before the Session (Consent)

Instead of: "Are you okay with being drawn?"
Clean: "And what would you like to have happen in this session?"

Instead of: "Tell me if you need a break"
Clean: "And when you need something, how will you let us know?"

### During the Session (Holding Space)

Instead of: "That's a beautiful pose"
Clean: "And that's like what?" (to the drawer, not the model)

Instead of: "Focus on the line of the back"
Clean: "And what are you noticing?"

### After the Session (Reflection)

Instead of: "Did you like how you were drawn?"
Clean: "And when you see [the drawing], what happens?"

Instead of: "You did a great job"
Clean: "And is there anything else about that experience?"

## CARDS in the Life Room

| CARD | Life Drawing Application |
|------|-------------------------|
| **Competence** | Skill is welcomed at all levels; no hierarchy of "good" or "bad" marks |
| **Autonomy** | Model chooses poses, breaks, visibility of face; drawer chooses medium, focus, interpretation |
| **Relatedness** | Mutual witnessing; "we're in this together"; the room as container |
| **Dignity** | Model's body is their own; no comments on body shape/size; marks respond, not evaluate |
| **Safety** | Physical (temperature, position, breaks); Psychological (no judgment, clear consent, exit available) |

### CARDS Diagnostic

```
If C in red → Are we making hierarchy from skill?
If A in red → Are we directing too much?
If R in red → Has the container broken? Is someone isolated?
If D in red → Has objectification crept in? Are we treating the model as object?
If S in red → Is someone physically uncomfortable? Psychologically exposed beyond consent?
```

## BODY Check Protocol

The body is the court of final appeal in life drawing.

### For Models

```
Routine: Model BODY Check

Before session:
  - Do I want to be here today? (consent check)
  - What's my energy level? (duration calibration)
  - What parts of me feel available to be seen? (boundary setting)

During session:
  - Am I comfortable? (adjust)
  - Am I present or dissociating? (ground)
  - Is this pose sustainable? (communicate)

After session:
  - How does my body feel now? (residue check)
  - Did I maintain my boundaries? (integrity check)
```

### For Drawers

```
Routine: Drawer BODY Check

Before session:
  - Where is my attention coming from? (intention check)
  - Am I here to witness or to consume? (ethics check)

During session:
  - Am I in my body or just my eyes/hands? (presence check)
  - What happens in my body when I make this mark? (response check)

After session:
  - How does my body feel about what I made? (somatic reflection)
```

### For Facilitator

```
Routine: Facilitator BODY Check

Before session:
  - Do I have capacity to hold this space? (resource check)
  - What's my own need for rescue/control? (drama-awareness check)

During session:
  - Am I holding or gripping? (Clean: minimal intervention)
  - Is my presence supporting or directing? (sovereignty check)

After session:
  - Did I rescue anyone? Did I need to? (pattern reflection)
```

## Drama Detection in Group Dynamics

### Early Warning Signs

| Signal | Possible Drama | Intervention |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Model frequently adjusts, seems uncomfortable | Victim position; consent may be compromised | Clean: "And what would you like to have happen?" |
| Drawer makes comments about model's body | Persecutor position; objectification creeping in | Remind: "We respond to what's there; we don't evaluate" |
| Facilitator overexplaining, over-holding | Rescuer position; may be creating dependency | Self-check: "Am I supporting capacity or preventing experience?" |
| Someone becomes very quiet | Possible withdrawal; Victim position | Clean: "And is there anything you need?" |
| Tension between participants | Drama triangle may be active | Map the triangle; acknowledge all positions |

### When Drama Emerges

1. **Pause** — break the automatic cycle
2. **Name** — "I notice something shifting in the room"
3. **Multi-partiality** — acknowledge all positions as valid
4. **Clean question** — "And what needs to happen?"
5. **Exit option** — "Anyone can step out at any time"

## Hosting Protocol (Draft)

### Opening Ritual

```
1. Soft Contract
   - Duration, breaks, modality
   - What's in scope, what's not
   - Exit always available

2. BODY Arrival
   - Brief somatic grounding
   - "Notice where you are; notice how you arrived"

3. Clean Frame
   - "We're here to witness, not to judge"
   - "The model is choosing to be seen; we're choosing to respond"
   - "Marks are responses, not evaluations"
```

### During Session

```
1. Minimal Intervention
   - Timekeeping without disruption
   - Temperature, comfort checks
   - No aesthetic direction unless requested

2. Clean Presence
   - Use exact words when reflecting
   - Ask, don't interpret
   - Trust the body (theirs and yours)

3. Drama Awareness
   - Watch for role-switches
   - Intervene early, lightly
   - Multi-partiality when tension rises
```

### Closing Ritual

```
1. BODY Departure
   - "Notice how you are now"
   - Transition time before leaving

2. Clean Reflection (Optional)
   - "And what happened for you?"
   - No interpretations, no corrections

3. Clean Brushes
   - Return room to neutral
   - Acknowledge what was held
```
