## §3. Source evaluation

### 3.1 The term: defined by Hugo, not coined by him

Hugo's stated purpose is to trace the typical forms of vernufpoësie in Afrikaans; the term must first be explained ("verklaar"), and he takes as his starting point Kannemeyer's 1977 description of J.C. Steyn's poetry as "'n fenomenologiese spel met die taal" (p. 1; restated p. 211). Nothing in the read pages claims the coinage. The term is already in circulation in his sources (Cloete on Steyn, 1976/1980, p. 22). **The essay's "coinage by Hugo not established" stands; the origin stays unattributed until Kannemeyer 1977 is read.**

### 3.2 The definition

> "die intellektuele spel met die versvorm en met die tipografiese, spellings-, fonologiese, morfologiese, sintaktiese, semantiese en etimologiese moontlikhede of skynmoontlikhede van die taal. Die medium word dus in 'n groot mate doel of tema op sigself." (p. 1; verbatim again p. 211)

Two qualifications Hugo attaches at once (p. 2): the difference from other poetry is one of **degree, not kind** ("eerder op 'n graadverskil as op 'n wesensverskil"), the criterion being the degree of concentration on language itself; and the study is **not a taxonomy in the natural-science sense**. The chapter-2 summary widens the definition to name both objects of play: "die intellektuele spel met die taal op al sy vlakke en met die poëtiese vorm" (p. 97).

### 3.3 Vernuf against *speels*, and the play element

Hugo rejects the synonym *geestige poësie* because Afrikaans criticism had used it confusingly (p. 2), and rejects Schutte's Addison-derived taxonomy of *geestigheid* (delight and surprise) as subjective and moralistic (pp. 4–5). His own cut: **vernufpoësie** ("poetry of wit", which may be serious) against **speelse poësie** ("light verse", his own translation, p. 4). What all vernufpoësie shares, "in erns en in luim", is the *spelelement* (p. 4). *Ernstige parodie* is his worked case of the serious kind (p. 97).

### 3.4 Theoretical orientation: Jakobson, intensified

Chapter 1.2 (pp. 6–13) sets out Jakobson's six factors and functions; the poetic function is attention to the message as message, the palpability of the sign. Vernufpoësie is that function to a still greater degree (p. 10), so that "Nog meer as in ander soorte poësie skep die vernufpoësie sy eie 'taal'" (p. 13), with Pienaar quoted on form and content becoming inseparable. The language techniques are grouped under **woordspel** after Schultink (klankspel; woorddeel-, woord-, woordgroep- and sin-speling; *herhalingswoordspel* against *ambigue woordspel*, pp. 14–17) and after Claes/Todorov on a paradigmatic × syntagmatic grid (klankspeling, woordspeling, klankkruising, woordkruising, pp. 18–20). The Opsomming's list of semantic phenomena: homonimie and syntactic homonimie, polisemie, paronimie, real and pseudo-morphological *verkapping*, real and *skyn*-etymology, homografie, homofonie, neologismes, anagramme, palindrome, metatesis, *betekenisverletterliking* (p. 212).

### 3.5 Verse techniques (§2.2.2, pp. 53–97)

Opens: "Vernuftigheid in die poësie kan hom ook manifesteer as 'n spel met die versvorm as sodanig" (p. 53), with the **apokoinou** ("double syntax", Cloete) as the hinge between language-level and verse-level play (p. 53). Then, as bold in-text terms rather than numbered sections: the limerick and *goëlrym* (trick rhyme, pp. 54–59); visual/figurative, iconic, concrete and acoustic poetry (pp. 60–73); **metapoëtikale ikone**, "waar die versvorm oor homself praat ... die kenmerke van 'n spesifieke digvorm word die onderwerp of tema van 'n gedig in daardie vorm" (p. 74), with Hugo's own quatrain as a worked example (p. 80); and **parodie**, defined as "die doelbewuste versteuring van die verhouding tussen vorm en inhoud" (p. 84), under the generic *burleske*. The Opsomming adds *apokoinou*, *goëlrym*, concrete/iconic verse, metapoetic icons and parody as the verse-form list (p. 212).

### 3.6 Constraint: generative, on a stated condition

Hugo never poses "generative or ornamental" in those words. His criterion is stated once, at the typography boundary: "Die betekenisinhoud van die woord bly primêr. Die drukbeeld het 'n semantiese funksie: dit help met die oordrag van 'n bewuste boodskap" (pp. 65–66); typography absolutised into "abstrakte tipografiese vormgewing wat alleen estetiese informasie oordra" has crossed "die grens na die grafiese kuns" (p. 66). Three further conditions: the parody counts as vernufpoësie only "wanneer die leser dit as 'n parodie herken het en die oorspronklike dus bygelees word" (p. 97); a single technique in isolation lacks "die kenmerkende digtheid en hoë graad van meerduidigheid" (p. 212); and the study's own evaluative practice reads rhyme scheme, lineation and even deviation from the scheme as meaning-bearing (p. 80). **Reading: form is generative for Hugo when it feeds back into meaning and is recognised by a reader; form that only carries aesthetic information is a boundary case, extra-literary at the limit.**

### 3.7 The hermetic question (ch. 3 opening, pp. 98–100)

Hugo chose Scholtz's *Grimas* "omdat die deur die kritiek gesinjaleerde kriptiek van Scholtz se poësie 'n besondere uitdaging aan die ondersoeker bied" (p. 99). Steyn's verse is "waarskynlik ingewikkelder ... maar nie so kripties nie" because it names its own techniques; "By Scholtz is die vernufspel gemaskeer" (p. 99). The boundary of vernufpoësie inside *Grimas* is drawn by the presence of the language devices, not by transparency (p. 100), and the poems outside it are not ranked lower: "hulle is net soseer skeppinge van Scholtz as sy vernuftigste vers" (p. 100). **Hermetic verse is inside vernufpoësie for Hugo, as its masked mode.**

### 3.8 The reader (ch. 4 opening, pp. 147–155)

Snyman, quoted approvingly: effective wordplay "toets taalvernuf, dit open onverwagte klank- en betekenismoontlikhede binne die taal" and is "essensieel 'n toets vir die taal self" (p. 147). Two costs are recorded rather than hidden: repetition of the same device across poems wears it down (Brink and Kannemeyer, p. 148), and one intertextual reference is so obscure that "Sonder 'n persoonlike mededeling van die digter sou hierdie leser (en ek glo geen ander leser) hierdie obskure verwysing na Reichling raakgesien het nie" (p. 154). The Opsomming's closing line: Steyn's verses "verg 'n sekere taalwetenskaplike kennis van die leser" (p. 213).

### 3.9 Feeling and reason (p. 21)

On Toerien's quatrain "Gesprekke met my hand": "'gevoelspoësie' nie sonder die rasionele element kan bestaan nie en andersom dat 'verstandverse' nie noodwendig van gevoel ... ontbloot hoef te wees nie" (p. 21). Directly on *Ways and Means*' head/heart argument; a Bronze anchor the essay may cite.

**No CARDS hard stop.** The live strain is Competence/Relatedness against the practice's inclusivity commitment, and it is the practice's to resolve, not Hugo's.

### 4.3 Known critiques / And-Yet material

- Hugo's frame is structuralist and 1988: Jakobson, Schultink, Claes. The reader appears as a condition (recognition, competence) rather than as a theorised party. Any reader-centred use of the term is the repo's addition and should say so.
- The corpus is Afrikaans only; the phenomenon is not (Hugo says so himself, p. 22: Huygens, Donne, the metaphysicals, the Vijftigers). The term travels; the taxonomy's examples do not.
- "Vernuf" carries a mastery register. Hugo's own reading of Toerien (p. 21) undoes the wit/feeling binary, and the repo should lean on that page rather than on the word.
- Hugo declines to give the term a hard edge (degree, not kind). A lib entry that hardens it would misrepresent him.

### 4.4 Integration hypothesis

Vernufpoësie names, in a critical tradition the operator writes inside, the practice the repo has been calling polysemiologism and the homonym as method. It supplies the taxonomy, the reader condition, and one criterion for when form-play carries meaning. It does not supply a theory of the reader, a warrant for the chapbook's subtitle, or any support for a vernuf-versus-hermetic distinction.

### 4.5 Draft utility statement

Vernufpoësie: poetry in which intellectual play with the verse form and with the possibilities of the language becomes, to a greater degree than elsewhere, the medium's own end (Hugo 1988, p. 1). A difference of degree, not kind. Imported narrowly, it gives the repo a lineage and a vocabulary for its form-play, and a test: the device must feed back into meaning and be recognisable by a reader.
