## Core Constructs (L0)

| Construct | Definition |
|-----------|------------|
| **Ethics Triad** | Three core ethics: **Earth Care** (stewardship of living systems), **People Care** (meeting human needs without exploitation), **Fair Share** (set limits to consumption, redistribute surplus). The axiological foundation; all design flows from these. |
| **Twelve Design Principles** | Holmgren's (2002) articulation: (1) Observe and interact, (2) Catch and store energy, (3) Obtain a yield, (4) Apply self-regulation and accept feedback, (5) Use and value renewable resources and services, (6) Produce no waste, (7) Design from patterns to details, (8) Integrate rather than segregate, (9) Use small and slow solutions, (10) Use and value diversity, (11) Use edges and value the marginal, (12) Creatively use and respond to change. |
| **Zone Planning** | Spatial organisation radiating outward from Zone 0 (home/self) through Zone 5 (wilderness/unmanaged). Intensity of management decreases with distance. A design heuristic for allocating attention, energy, and frequency of interaction. |
| **Sector Analysis** | Mapping external energies (sun, wind, water, fire, noise, pollution) flowing through a site. Sectors are forces you manage *around*, not *against*. |
| **Stacking Functions** | Each design element serves multiple purposes; each important function is supported by multiple elements. Redundancy through diversity. |
| **Guilds** | Assemblages of species that mutually benefit one another — the polyculture unit. Modelled on observed ecological relationships. |
| **Edge Effect** | Interfaces between systems (forest/meadow, land/water) are the most productive and diverse zones. Design to maximise productive edge. |
| **Succession** | Designing with natural succession in mind — working *with* temporal processes rather than freezing systems at a single stage. |
| **Yield** | The sum of useful outputs from a system. "The yield of a system is theoretically unlimited, or only limited by the imagination and information of the designer." (Mollison) |
| **Permanent Culture** | The full expansion: not merely permanent *agriculture* but permanent *culture* — sustainable human settlements integrating food, shelter, energy, community, and governance. |

**Central thesis:** Design is the critical link between ethics and action. Permaculture provides a design system — grounded in ecological ethics and informed by observation of natural patterns — for creating productive, resilient, and equitable human settlements. The designer's task is not to impose order but to arrange elements so that their relationships generate emergent yields.

## Source Evaluation (Phase 3)

### SE-1: Ferguson & Lovell (2014) — Gold

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Key Claims** | (1) Permaculture is simultaneously a design system, a movement, a set of practices, and a worldview. (2) The movement has been largely isolated from mainstream agricultural science. (3) Core concepts (guilds, polyculture, zone/sector) have scientific analogues but are rarely cited in peer-reviewed literature. (4) Permaculture's design dimension — the integration of elements into wholes — is its distinctive contribution beyond what agroecology offers. |
| **Methodology** | Systematic literature review combining bibliometric analysis with qualitative thematic coding. Reviews 92 works (peer-reviewed + grey literature). The foundational academic review of the field. |
| **Relevance** | **Very High.** Provides the evidential landscape map for the entire brief. The "four faces" framing (design, movement, practice, worldview) is a valuable diagnostic for understanding what permaculture *is* and why it resists simple categorisation. The isolation-from-science finding is essential context for And-Yet material. |
| **Notes** | Open access (Springer/HAL). Most-cited academic paper on permaculture. Ferguson's subsequent empirical work (2017, 2019) is built on this foundation. L1 priority citation. |

### SE-2: Krebs & Bach (2018) — Gold

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Key Claims** | (1) All twelve Holmgren design principles have scientific evidence supporting them. (2) Permaculture principles overlap substantially with but are not identical to agroecological principles. (3) Permaculture's distinctive contribution is the *design and implementation* layer — the integration methodology, not the individual techniques. |
| **Methodology** | Principle-by-principle literature review mapping each of Holmgren's twelve principles to peer-reviewed evidence. Systematic but not meta-analytic. |
| **Relevance** | **Very High.** Directly validates the twelve principles against scientific literature — essential for establishing that the design system has empirical grounding. Confirms that the principles are not merely practitioner folklore but correspond to documented ecological mechanisms. Strengthens the case for permaculture as a rigorous design methodology. |
| **Notes** | Gold OA (MDPI Sustainability). The principle-by-principle structure is directly useful for the lib entry's Core Constructs section. Limitation: validation of principles individually does not prove they function as an integrated system. |

### SE-3: Schmid et al. (2024) — Gold

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Key Claims** | (1) Permaculture sites show 27% higher soil organic carbon, 20% lower bulk density, 201% higher earthworm abundance, 457% higher vascular plant richness, and 197% higher bird species richness compared to conventional controls. | **Methodology** | Empirical paired-comparison study. Quantitative measurements across multiple ecological indicators. Published in *Communications Earth & Environment* (Nature portfolio). The most rigorous empirical study of permaculture outcomes to date. |
| **Relevance** | **High.** Provides the strongest empirical evidence that permaculture design produces measurable ecological outcomes. #### §4.2 Poetry and Artistry

Permaculture's aesthetic register is distinctive: it is a *practitioner's poetry*, not an academic's or a philosopher's. Mollison's prose is aphoristic and earthy — "Though the problems of the world are increasingly complex, the solutions remain embarrassingly simple." Holmgren's twelve principles carry a mnemonic elegance that belies their systemic depth. The zone model is a spatial poem — a radiating mandala of attention. The guild is a social sculpture in ecology. The design system's beauty lies in its *double reading*: every principle operates simultaneously as ecological instruction and ethical injunction. **Aesthetic resonance: high — grounded, not ethereal.**

### Draft Utility Statement

> Permaculture provides a **design methodology that operationalises ethical principles as ecological and social practice** — filling a gap between the repository's axiological frameworks (CARDS, Salutogenesis) and its design vocabularies (Parametric Authorship, Wardley Maps). It offers:
>
> 1. **Ethics-to-design bridge:** The ethics triad (Earth Care, People Care, Fair Share) translates axiological commitments into design constraints. Every principle is simultaneously an ecological instruction and an ethical injunction.
> 2. **Spatial design vocabulary:** Zone planning, sector analysis, and edge-effect thinking provide a spatial grammar absent from the current library — a way of organising attention, energy, and interaction by proximity and intensity.
> 3. **Temporal design vocabulary:** Succession thinking and yield-over-time introduce a temporal dimension to design — working *with* natural processes rather than freezing systems at a single stage.
> 4. **Stacking functions as integration heuristic:** Each element serves multiple purposes; each important function is served by multiple elements. This is the operational grammar of redundancy, resilience, and yield.
> 5. **Empirical grounding for regenerative design:** Schmid et al. (2024) provide measurable evidence that permaculture design produces emergent ecological outcomes beyond the sum of individual techniques.
> 6. **Salutogenic affordance creation:** Stoltz & Schaffer (2018) demonstrate empirically that permaculture design creates the conditions for health-creation (SOC components) — directly bridging SALUTOGENESIS and REGENERATIVE_CULTURES through practice.
> 7. **Parametric authorship instantiation:** The permaculture designer as parametric author — setting constraints (zones, guilds, edge ratios) within which the system generates its own yields. Design ≠ determination; design = conditions for emergence.
