## Core Constructs (L0)

| Construct | Definition |
|-----------|------------|
| **Regenerative (vs. sustainable)** | Sustainability = doing less damage (harm reduction). Regeneration = actively restoring and improving natural and social systems. Net positive, not net zero. |
| **Salutogenic design** | Term coined by Wahl & Baxter (2008): design as a health-creating practice across scales — from personal to planetary. Extends Antonovsky. |
| **Scale-linking design** | Design interventions must connect across scales (individual → community → bioregion → planet). No single scale suffices. |
| **Questions-based framework** | The book is structured around questions, not prescriptions. Regenerative cultures are place-adapted, so methodology is inquiry-driven. |
| **Biocultural uniqueness of place** | Regenerative cultures are necessarily plural — they emerge from specific ecological, social, cultural conditions. Opposes universalising approaches. |
| **Crisis of perception** | Root cause of ecological crisis is epistemological: the erroneous separation of nature and culture (via Bateson, Capra). |
| **Appropriate participation** | Rather than controlling nature, design should enable humans to participate in natural processes (biomimicry vs. bionics). |
| **Whole-systems / living-systems thinking** | Autopoiesis, Santiago Theory of Cognition, self-organisation. Living systems are self-creating; cultures must align. |

**Central thesis:** Converging crises are rooted in a "crisis of perception" — the separation of nature from culture. The response is not sustainability (inadequate) but the active design of *regenerative* cultures: diverse, place-adapted, life-affirming ways of living that restore ecological, social, and personal health.
