## §3. Source Evaluation

### 3.1 Martin & Dalzen (2021) — Primary Source

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Key claims** | Two independent axes (who does / who it's for); four quadrants; "want to" vs. "willing to" distinction; consent as collaborative agreement |
| **Methodology** | Inductively derived from 30+ years of bodywork practice + 3-Minute Game observation. No formal research methodology. |
| **Relevance** | High — sole systematic account of the framework |
| **Limitations** | Self-published (Luminare Press). No peer review. No empirical validation. No control studies. |

### 3.2 Faden & Beauchamp (1986) — Ancestor Source

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Key claims** | Consent requires autonomous authorisation, not mere acquiescence. Distinguishes institutional/effective consent from genuine autonomous consent. |
| **Mapping to Wheel** | Martin's "want to" vs. "willing to" ≈ Faden & Beauchamp's autonomous authorisation vs. effective consent. Martin's observation that people "allow" rather than speak up = bioethical concern about mere compliance. |
| **Relevance** | Strongest theoretical ancestor. Provides Gold-tier grounding for the Wheel's core distinction. |
| **Limitation** | Martin does not cite this work. The connection is our reconstruction, not her claimed lineage. |

### 3.3 Dunkley & Brotto (2020) — Ancestor Source

| Dimension | Assessment |
|-----------|-----------|
| **Key claims** | BDSM communities have developed sophisticated consent practices (SSC, RACK) that prioritise explicit negotiation, ongoing communication, and revocability. |
| **Mapping to Wheel** | The 3-Minute Game was literally created for a BDSM workshop. The Wheel descends directly from this consent negotiation culture. |
| **Relevance** | Establishes academic credibility for the community practices the Wheel formalises. |

## §4. Synthesis

### 4.1 CARDS Alignment (Axiology First)

| CARDS Axis | Wheel of Consent Alignment | Assessment |
|------------|---------------------------|------------|
| **C** (Competence) | Teaches consent literacy — the skill of knowing what you want and asking for it. Builds mastery in relational clarity. | Strong |
| **A** (Autonomy) | **Core mechanic.** The Wheel is fundamentally an autonomy-operationalisation tool. Each quadrant requires knowing your own desire and making a choice. | Exceptional |
| **R** (Relatedness) | Creates connection through negotiated, transparent relating. Builds belonging through mutual consent. | Strong |
| **D** (Dignity) | Treats every person as a subject with agency, preferences, and boundaries worth respecting. | Strong |
| **S** (Safety) | Touch only happens within explicit negotiation. Safety is foundational, not supplementary. | Strong |

**No CARDS threats detected.** The framework structurally serves all five axes.

### 4.2 Aesthetic Resonance (Poetry Second)

The Wheel is elegantly simple — two axes, four quadrants. The language is accessible without being simplistic. The 3-Minute Game is a powerful experiential device. The "want to" vs. "willing to" distinction carries immediate felt resonance for anyone who has said yes when they meant maybe.

### 4.3 Technical Soundness (Ethos Third)

| Check | Assessment |
|-------|-----------|
| Methodology transparent? | Yes — inductively derived, process documented |
| Claims falsifiable? | Core claims are experiential/phenomenological, not empirical. The two-axis model is a conceptual framework, not a testable hypothesis. |
| Author accountable? | Yes — published, contactable, active teaching programme |
| Known critiques addressed? | Limited — no formal academic critique exists to address |

### 4.4 Integration Hypothesis

The Wheel of Consent would:
1. **Operationalise** what OBJECTIFICATION theorises about benign consent conditions
2. **Complement** MULTI_PARTIALITY's give-take balance with a somatic/embodied dimension
3. **Ground** the Philosophy of Body's "sovereign boundary" commitment in concrete practice
4. **Provide theoretical framing** for Multi-Party Encounter protocol elements (heat-check, Quiet Tap-Out)
5. **Fill the gap** between CARDS' abstract autonomy axis and its embodied expression in touch contexts

### 4.5 Draft Utility Statement

The Wheel of Consent provides a practitioner-derived framework for operationalising consent in embodied, relational, and facilitation contexts. It fills a specific gap: the repo has consent theory (OBJECTIFICATION), consent ethics (MULTI_PARTIALITY), and consent infrastructure (CONSENT_LEDGER), but no somatic consent framework that operationalises these in the domain of touch and embodied interaction.
